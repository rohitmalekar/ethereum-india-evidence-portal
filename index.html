Ethereum and Distributed Settlement Infrastructure in Indian Institutional Finance

What this is: A six-module evidence base on where distributed-ledger settlement and tokenisation stand for Indian institutional finance, built for readers who have seen several blockchain pitches this year and were unconvinced by all of them. Every claim carries its source and date. Every figure carries a source tier. It is written to be checkable rather than persuasive.

The 30-second position

Tokenisation is real but narrow: cash, money-market funds and repo settle on-chain in production today, mostly on permissioned ledgers, while bonds, equities and trade finance remain pilots or failures. India already runs one production DLT system in regulated finance (SEBI's covenant-monitoring platform) and two nascent central-bank-settled pilots, so the domestic question is which process is worth the organisational cost, not whether the technology works.

The reconciled reading of the evidence, stated plainly: Ethereum leads the global asset-tokenisation layer by value, but is the wrong rail for almost every domestic Indian core-settlement use case. Confidentiality, settlement finality and monetary sovereignty all point to permissioned or CBDC rails at home. Public Ethereum's defensible role in India is narrow: GIFT City fund tokenisation, cross-border flows, and, once the VDA tax classification is clarified, unit tracking inside an already-regulated domestic fund wrapper. The institutional case is about infrastructure, not about holding any asset, a distinction Indian regulators actively draw and one this evidence base holds to throughout.

Reading guide and scope

This portal is a working research artifact, published openly so its sources can be checked. The summary tiers reflect the reconciliation pass; the full module reports are as originally researched. See the Reconciliation tab for every place two modules diverged and which figure to rely on.

The six modules.

  • A — What Actually Shipped. What blockchain and Ethereum-based infrastructure is genuinely in production globally, at what volume, on which chains, and why each architectural choice was made. Includes the graveyard of failed institutional projects.
  • B — India's Actual Perimeter. What is legally and practically permitted in India today for tokenised instruments, distributed settlement and blockchain registries, separating what is barred from what is merely unaddressed.
  • C — Where the Value Actually Is. Which specific Indian financial processes are expensive or slow enough that a settlement or tokenisation primitive would measurably help, sized in rupees, ranked against how far each sits from a permitted pilot.
  • D — The Honest Comparison. How Ethereum compares to its realistic alternatives (other public chains, permissioned ledgers, and no blockchain at all) on the criteria institutions actually use, including where Ethereum is the wrong answer.
  • E — The Objection Bank. The strongest objections a skeptical Indian institutional or regulatory audience will raise, each in its strongest form, with the honest evidence-based response and the residual weakness that remains.
  • F — Adoption Precedent and the Devcon Question. How new financial infrastructure has historically crossed into adoption in India (demat, UPI, Aadhaar, Account Aggregator, ONDC, RTGS), and what that implies for how a tokenised layer would have to be introduced, plus what a developer conference can and cannot achieve.

How to read the modules. Each module opens with three tiers: a 30-second answer, a 5-minute summary, and the full report. The summary tiers carry the corrections from the reconciliation pass; the full reports are the modules as originally researched, with a note where a figure or framing was later revised. The Figure Ledger is the reusable spine, holding every quantitative claim with its source, date and tier, filterable and staleness-flagged. When two modules disagreed on a number, the Reconciliation tab records which to rely on. Nothing here should be lifted into an external document without checking that tab and the ledger first.

Source tiers, used on every figure: T1 primary (regulator, statute, filing, on-chain); T2 named data provider (RWA.xyz, DefiLlama, World Bank, BIS); T3 tier-1 financial press; T4 company blog or press release (promotional); T5 crypto media or aggregator (leads only, never a sole source).

Status labels, on every initiative: IN PRODUCTION (live, with a volume figure and date); LIMITED PILOT (live but restricted, named participants); ANNOUNCED ONLY (no evidence of live use); WOUND DOWN (discontinued, with date and reason).

A standing caveat. This subject moves monthly. Roughly a fifth of the figures in this base rest on the weakest two source tiers, and many carry an "as of" date more than six months old. The Figure Ledger flags both. Re-check anything flagged before it reaches an external document.
