# Module B — India's Actual Perimeter: What Is Legally and Practically Permitted for Tokenised Finance, Distributed Settlement and Blockchain Registries

## TL;DR
- India already runs one blockchain/DLT system in genuine production in regulated finance — SEBI's mandatory Security and Covenant Monitoring System for corporate bonds, operated by NSDL and CDSL and gating ISIN activation since April 2022 — while every other tokenisation initiative (RBI's tokenised CD pilot on UMI, SEBI's corporate-bond tokenisation pilot, IFSCA's RWA framework) is at pilot or announced-only stage.
- The single most important analytical point: for tokenised *securities* and tokenised *deposits*, the binding constraint in India is almost never a prohibition. It is UNADDRESSED legal ground (settlement finality, ledger-as-register recognition, smart-contract enforceability) plus supervisory caution. Only private crypto-asset banking friction and the punitive VDA tax regime are genuinely restrictive — and those largely do not touch the institutional tokenisation case, which the sources repeatedly conflate.
- The widest permitted perimeter is not the domestic market but GIFT City/IFSC, where the IFSCA (TechFin and Ancillary Services) Regulations, 2025 already license the technology layer and a sandbox carries live tokenisation platforms. The gap between India and the Module A precedents is therefore mostly a gap of *enabling legal instruments* (an EU DLT-Pilot / UK DSS-style disapplication power) that no Indian body currently holds outside IFSC.

## Key Findings

**1. India has exactly one DLT system in production in mainstream regulated finance, and it is a registry, not a settlement or token system.**
FINDING: SEBI mandated a "Security and Covenant Monitoring System" using Distributed Ledger Technology via Circular SEBI/HO/MIRSD/CRADT/CIR/P/2022/38 dated 29 March 2022, effective for all non-convertible securities issued on or after 1 April 2022; the platform is built and operated by the depositories NSDL and CDSL, and ISIN activation is gated on the DLT recording. Adoption is now near-complete for the secured-debt universe: per platform vendor Cognizant's published case study, 4,291 issuers have been onboarded, 6,368 secured ISINs have been mapped with assets by the issuer (covering almost 90% of outstanding secured ISINs), and mapping has been approved by debenture trustees for 6,366 secured ISINs (more than 95% of ISINs). STATUS: IN PRODUCTION. [T1: SEBI circular, sebi.gov.in/legal/circulars/mar-2022/operational-guidelines-for-security-and-covenant-monitoring-using-distributed-ledger-technology-dlt-_57331.html, 29 Mar 2022; T4: Cognizant case study, cognizant.com.] For scale context, NSDL — the operator — had 79,773 registered issuers as of 31 March 2025 (6,287 listed; 73,486 unlisted), per its IPO note/RHP (Axis Capital, Jul 2025). This is a permissioned DLT recording charges, covenants and asset cover — not a public chain and not a settlement rail. It demonstrates Indian regulators will mandate DLT where it sits *inside* the existing depository perimeter and touches no settlement-finality or property-transfer question.

**2. RBI is running a tokenised deposit-instrument pilot, but it is wholesale, closed, and legally hedged by its own officials.**
FINDING: RBI's FY26 Annual Report (released 29 May 2026) states it "developed the Unified Markets Interface (UMI), a multi-layer platform to facilitate tokenisation of financial assets while leveraging wholesale CBDC to enhance settlement efficiencies," and that "a pilot for the issuance and trading of certificates of deposit in tokenised form has begun on the platform, with settlement taking place through wholesale CBDC." The pilot launched on wholesale-CBDC rails (e₹-W) on 8 October 2025, with tokens "backed one-to-one by actual deposits," and involves "a few banks" covering interbank transfer/settlement, per RBI Chief General Manager (Fintech) Suvendu Pati (Business Standard / FinanceFeeds, 7-8 Oct 2025). STATUS: LIMITED PILOT. [T1/T3: RBI Annual Report FY26 via Business Standard, 29 May 2026; Moneycontrol/Business Standard interview with Suvendu Pati, 7 Oct 2025.] Pati explicitly hedged the legal foundation: "From a regulatory standpoint for tokenisation of an underlying asset, we believe integrity and enforceability have to be established... Risks in asset tokenisation are manageable and can be addressed through regulatory guardrails," adding that "there needs to be legal changes put in place... clarity on ownership rights."
INDIAN ANALOGUE: This is the direct analogue to Module A's tokenised money-market instruments (BlackRock BUIDL, Franklin BENJI) and JPMorgan Kinexys deposit tokens, but issued by banks and settled in wholesale CBDC rather than a commercial stablecoin.
WHO WOULD OWN IT: RBI Fintech Department (UMI/CBDC), with participating banks' treasury and financial-markets desks; the operator role is undefined.
WHAT BLOCKS SCALING TODAY: No statutory recognition that an on-chain token *is* the CD (versus a mirror of a depository/physical record), and no settlement-finality statute covering on-chain DvP. RBI officials named this explicitly.
WHAT WOULD HAVE TO CHANGE: Amendments recognising tokenised instruments and on-chain finality — RBI Act / Payment and Settlement Systems Act 2007 changes for finality; Depositories Act or a bespoke instrument for register status.
CONFIDENCE: High that the pilot is live; medium on scope, since RBI has named only "a few banks" and published no volumes.

**3. SEBI's corporate-bond tokenisation is announced, not shipped — and SEBI has said so.**
FINDING: SEBI Chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey announced on 26 May 2026 (CareEdge Debt Market Summit) that SEBI "decided to initiate" a pilot on use of DLT for tokenisation of corporate bonds, with implementation expected in "six to nine months"; he repeated the 6-9-month timeline at the ICICI Securities conference on 8 June 2026. SEBI's own Annual Report 2025-26 (Aug 2026) lists the corporate-bond tokenisation pilot among priorities for 2026-27 "without providing a further timeline." STATUS: ANNOUNCED ONLY. [T3: Business Standard, 26 May 2026; T5: SEBI Annual Report 2025-26 via CryptoTimes, 17 Aug 2026.] Pandey was careful to note DLT is "already being used in certain areas such as depositories and covenant monitoring" (Finding 1) and that corporate bonds "are already traded under existing systems" — the pilot tests whether tokenisation adds settlement/liquidity value, not whether it is legal.

**4. The widest permitted perimeter is GIFT City/IFSC, and the enabling instrument already exists there.**
FINDING: The IFSCA (TechFin and Ancillary Services) Regulations, 2025, notified 8 July 2025, superseded the 2022 FinTech Entity Framework; the Second Schedule lists 24 permitted TechFin services expressly including "Blockchain, DLT, Web 3.0 and Tokenisation." A technology provider (tooling, smart-contract issuance engines, whitelisting, cap-table administration) can obtain a Certificate of Registration today. Actual *issuance or management* of tokenised assets has no completed licence category — IFSCA published a consultation paper "Regulatory Approach towards Tokenization of Real-World Assets" on 26 February 2025 that posed questions rather than rules — so live token-platform operators run under the sandbox. STATUS: PERMITTED (technology layer, CoR) / SANDBOX ONLY (issuance/platform). [T1: IFSCA TAS Regulations 2025, ifsca.gov.in; T1: IFSCA consultation paper, 26 Feb 2025, ifsca.gov.in; T4: PKM Advisory analysis, 3 Aug 2026.] IFSCA has given conditional sandbox approval to platform operators including Realdom India Private Limited (Pinvest Exchange, fractional real-estate tokens) and Terazo. The IFSCA FinTech Sandbox Framework of 16 March 2026 now requires applicants to first classify their activity against the TAS Regulations before applying.
INDIAN ANALOGUE: IFSC is India's onshore-offshore analogue to Singapore's MAS Project Guardian perimeter and the ADGM/DIFC free zones.
WHO WOULD OWN IT: IFSCA (unified regulator for GIFT-IFSC), covering fund managers (FME regime), custodians and exchanges (India INX, NSE IX).
WHAT BLOCKS DOMESTIC TRANSPLANT: IFSCA's powers derive from the IFSCA Act 2019, which applies only inside the IFSC. Nothing equivalent lets RBI/SEBI disapply domestic law for a pilot.
WHAT WOULD HAVE TO CHANGE: A domestic enabling statute or a delegated sandbox power (see comparative section).
CONFIDENCE: High on the TechFin CoR route and sandbox precedents; medium on scale, as IFSCA has not published tokenised AUM figures.

**5. There is no crypto ban — but there is no crypto law either, and the discussion paper is stuck.**
FINDING: The RBI's 6 April 2018 circular barring banks from serving crypto businesses was struck down by the Supreme Court on 4 March 2020 in *Internet and Mobile Association of India v. RBI* (WP(C) 528/2018) on proportionality grounds; it has not been reinstated. The long-promised inter-ministerial crypto discussion paper (DEA-led, with RBI and SEBI) has been delayed repeatedly since 2024 and was reported shelved as of April 2026, with RBI opposition cited as the main factor. STATUS: banking access PERMITTED (ban set aside); comprehensive policy UNADDRESSED. [T3: azbpartners.com / Lexology on IAMAI v. RBI, 2020; T5: Moneycontrol via CryptoTimes, 1 Apr 2026.] RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra reiterated on 6 June 2025 that crypto "can hamper financial stability and monetary policy." Crucially, the Parliamentary Standing Committee on Finance, examining the draft Securities Markets Code 2025, noted the Code is "technology-neutral," allowing "tokenised versions of existing securities such as shares, bonds and units of investment schemes to remain within the ambit of securities regulation" — a signal that tokenised *securities* are treated as securities, not as crypto.

**6. The VDA tax regime is punitive — but tokenised securities and tokenised deposits most likely fall outside it.**
FINDING: Under Section 115BBH of the Income-tax Act, income from transfer of a Virtual Digital Asset is taxed at a flat 30% plus 4% cess, with no deduction except cost of acquisition, no loss set-off (against other VDAs or any income), and no carry-forward; Section 194S imposes 1% TDS on transfers above ₹10,000 (₹50,000 for specified persons). Budget 2026 left this unchanged; Section 285BAA reporting for exchanges takes effect 1 April 2026. STATUS of the asset case: effectively BARRED-by-tax for institutions. [T1: Income-tax Act ss.115BBH, 194S, 2(47A); T3/T4: multiple tax-advisory summaries, 2026.] The VDA definition in Section 2(47A) turns on being a crypto-token/code that is not Indian currency or foreign currency. A tokenised CD or a tokenised corporate bond, being the same legal security/deposit as its conventional twin, should be taxed as that instrument — not as a VDA — but this has not been expressly carved out by CBDT, so the risk is classificatory. On GST: in practice 18% GST applies to services provided by crypto platforms (trading commission, custody, withdrawal), while the underlying asset transfer sits outside GST as crypto is classified as neither "goods" nor "services"; a 28% rate on transaction value was deliberated by the CBIC/GST law committee in 2022 but never enacted. [T3: Business Standard, 17 Feb 2022 and 28 Oct 2022; T5: tax-advisory blogs on a reported July 2025 18%-on-services clarification, which I could not confirm against a primary CBIC notification.]
INDIAN ANALOGUE: The tax wall is why an Indian institution cannot economically run a BUIDL-style or PYUSD-style product domestically on a public chain — the instrument would risk the 30%/1%-TDS treatment.
WHAT WOULD HAVE TO CHANGE: A CBDT clarification that tokenised securities/deposits are taxed as the underlying instrument, or a statutory carve-out in the VDA definition.
CONFIDENCE: High on the rates and mechanics; medium on the classification of institutional tokenised instruments, which is untested.

**7. The legal foundations are partially built: property yes, electronic-record admissibility yes, smart-contract enforceability broadly yes — but settlement finality and ledger-as-register are UNADDRESSED.**
FINDING: The Madras High Court held on 25 October 2025 in *Rhutikumari v. Zanmai Labs Pvt. Ltd.* (O.A. No. 194 of 2025, Justice N. Anand Venkatesh) that cryptocurrency "is not a tangible property nor is it a currency" but "is a property, which is capable of being enjoyed and possessed... and capable of being held in trust" — the first Indian judgment to expressly grant crypto property status, imposing fiduciary/trust duties on exchanges. [T3/T4: Cyril Amarchand Mangaldas client alert, 13 Nov 2025; medianama.com, 27 Oct 2025.] Separately, under the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 (ss. 61-63, replacing the Evidence Act's 65A/65B), electronic and digital records are admissible and carry "the same legal effect, validity and enforceability as other document," subject to the Section 63(4) certificate. STATUS: property recognition — PERMITTED/settled at HC level; evidentiary status — PERMITTED with the s.63 certificate; on-chain settlement finality — UNADDRESSED (no statute recognises on-chain finality; the Payment and Settlement Systems Act 2007 governs designated systems only); ledger-as-legal-register — UNADDRESSED (Depositories Act contemplates a depository, not a distributed ledger). The DPDP Act 2023 right to erasure (s.12) and consent-withdrawal (s.6(4)) sit in direct tension with immutable ledgers holding personal data, with penalties up to ₹250 crore per contravention — a design constraint, not a bar.

## Details

### Reserve Bank of India

- **CBDC (wholesale and retail).** The wholesale pilot began November 2022 and retail (e₹-R) on 1 December 2022. The retail user base "increased to six million, with engagement increasing to 17 banks by March 2025" (RBI Annual Report 2024-25). By 5 December 2025, Deputy Governor T. Rabi Sankar reported retail e-rupee transactions had reached about 120 million with value crossing ₹28,000 crore and "a little more than 8 million users"; by 2 July 2026, Governor Malhotra cited roughly 12 million users and "more than 175 million transactions... close to ₹400 billion" (~$4.5 billion at ~₹88/USD, mid-2026). Note the sharp gap between cumulative transaction *flow* and *stock in circulation*: e-Rupee in circulation was only ₹1,016 crore (~$115 million) as of March 2025 (RBI Annual Report FY26). On 8 October 2025 RBI launched a CBDC retail sandbox opening APIs to fintechs (announced by Governor Malhotra at Global Fintech Fest 2025). STATUS: LIMITED PILOT (retail and wholesale both remain "pilot"). [T1/T3: RBI Annual Reports FY25/FY26; Business Standard, 5 Dec 2025; Angel One, 2 Jul 2026.] Programmable CBDC has been used in FY26 for direct-benefit-transfer food subsidies in Gujarat, Puducherry and Chandigarh, redeemable only for eligible goods — a live example of programmable payments. [T1/T3: RBI Annual Report FY26 via Telangana Today/Business Standard, 29 May 2026.]
- **Legal gap RBI itself named.** Deputy Governor T. Rabi Sankar has said CBDC requires "consequential amendments... in the Coinage Act, Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA) and Information Technology Act" — confirming even the central bank's own token needs statutory work. [T3: Business Standard.]
- **Banking access for digital-asset businesses.** Post-2020, banks cannot cite the set-aside 2018 circular to refuse service; but supervisory "informal discouragement" persists and is a commercial, not legal, obstacle.

### SEBI

- **DLT covenant monitoring (Finding 1).** The mandate chain: framework circular SEBI/HO/MIRSD/MIRSD_CRADT/CIR/P/2021/618 (13 Aug 2021); operational guidelines SEBI/HO/MIRSD/CRADT/CIR/P/2022/38 (29 Mar 2022, effective 1 Apr 2022); extensions in May, Aug, Oct 2022 and Jan 2023 (the Jan 2023 circular mandated depositories to report non-compliance quarterly). NSDL's platform was unveiled 7 May 2022 by then-Chairperson Madhabi Puri Buch. Mechanics: each pledged asset gets a 12-digit unique asset ID with duplicate checks to prevent double-charging; issuers upload the Debenture Trust Deed and covenants; debenture trustees verify against Sub-Registrar/ROC/CERSAI/IBBI-IU sources; CRAs record ratings; ISINs are gated on the recording. [T1: SEBI circulars; T3: Business Standard, 29 Mar 2022.]
- **Tokenisation pilot (Finding 3):** ANNOUNCED ONLY.
- **DEMAT / depository framework.** SEBI continues incremental demat reform (e.g., Oct 2025 consultation easing transfer/demat of pre-2019 physical shares) but there is no proposal to replace the depository book-entry model with a DLT register. A DLT register is not currently *recognised* as the legal register — the Depositories Act 1996 contemplates a depository.
- **SRO / IOSCO.** SEBI is a member of IOSCO's Tokenisation Working Group and Vice-Chair of its Quantum Computing Working Group. No tokenisation SRO exists.

### IFSCA and GIFT City (the operational core)

Enumerated perimeter, per IFSCA TAS Regulations 2025 and sandbox framework:
- **Technology/tooling:** PERMITTED today via TechFin CoR (Second Schedule: Blockchain, DLT, Web 3.0, Tokenisation). Both provider and client must be non-resident from non-FATF-high-risk jurisdictions; Principal and Compliance Officers must be IFSC-based; USD reporting.
- **Token issuance / platform operation / asset management:** SANDBOX ONLY (Limited Use Authorisation), pending the RWA tokenisation framework. Live precedents: Realdom/Pinvest, Terazo.
- **Fund structures:** Fund Management Entity (FME) regime available; tokenisation can be the technology wrapper under an FME.
- **Custody, foreign-currency settlement, participant eligibility:** IFSC operates in foreign currency with non-resident participants; this is precisely what the domestic regime cannot replicate under FEMA.
- IFSCA publicly *denied* (via GIFT/cafemutual) media claims it was about to regulate crypto exchanges — the framework is RWA tokenisation, not crypto trading.

### MeitY, DEA, Ministry of Finance

- Crypto discussion paper: repeatedly delayed (promised 2023, Sept 2024, June/July 2025), reported shelved April 2026. DEA Secretary Ajay Seth said in Feb 2025 the paper was being "recalibrated" because "several countries changed their position." STATUS: UNADDRESSED at policy level.
- Parliamentary Standing Committee on Finance: recommended broader consultation before regulating crypto under the Securities Markets Code 2025; noted the Code is technology-neutral and covers tokenised securities but that crypto lacking securities characteristics falls outside it.

### FIU-IND and PMLA/VASP

- VDA service providers became "reporting entities" under PMLA via the 7 March 2023 MoF notification; FIU-IND registration is mandatory (FINnet/FINGate portal), extending to offshore exchanges serving Indian users. Enforcement: FIU-IND imposed an ₹18.82 crore penalty (~$2.25m at ~₹83/USD, Jun 2024) on Binance for PMLA violations in June 2024. [T4/T5: Compliance7, 2026; T3 corroboration on Binance penalty.]
- The FATF-style "control or influence" test for who is a VASP is embedded in the PMLA notification's activity list (exchange, transfer, safekeeping, administration, issuance participation); non-custodial software providers that never take "control" of assets are, on the face of the notification, outside it — but this is untested and supervisory risk is high.

### Tax (Finding 6)

Full VDA stack: 30% (s.115BBH) + 4% cess; 1% TDS (s.194S); no set-off/carry-forward; only acquisition cost deductible; Schedule VDA reporting; Section 285BAA exchange reporting from 1 Apr 2026. GST: 18% on platform services in practice; asset transfer outside GST; 28%-on-value was deliberated (2022) but not enacted.

### Legal foundations (Finding 7)

- **Smart contracts:** enforceable in principle — the Indian Contract Act 1872 is medium-neutral, and the IT Act 2000 recognises electronic records/signatures; a coded agreement meeting offer/acceptance/consideration/lawful-object tests is a contract. No case has voided a contract for being coded. UNADDRESSED specifically: automatic/self-executing enforcement where code and natural-language terms diverge.
- **Property:** *Rhutikumari* (Madras HC, 25 Oct 2025) — persuasive, not binding nationwide; a single-judge HC decision.
- **Evidence:** BSA 2023 ss.61-63 — admissible with s.63(4) certificate.
- **Settlement finality:** UNADDRESSED. PSS Act 2007 finality attaches to RBI-designated systems only.
- **DPDP interaction:** erasure/consent-withdrawal vs immutability — unresolved design tension; off-chain personal data with on-chain hashes is the standard workaround.

## Comparative Jurisdictions — the specific enabling mechanism (not "friendliness")

For each: the mechanism, then the one most transplantable into Indian law and the Indian instrument that would carry it.

- **Singapore (MAS, Project Guardian).** Mechanism: *no new carve-out*. Tokenised instruments are issued under existing securities law (Securities and Futures Act 2001) and the Payment Services Act; the Payment and Settlement Systems (Finality and Netting) Act 2002 supplies settlement finality for designated systems. Participants use existing licences. Most transplantable: extending an existing *settlement-finality statute* to a designated DLT system. Indian instrument: amend the Payment and Settlement Systems Act 2007 to let RBI designate a DLT settlement system as final.
- **EU (DLT Pilot Regime, Reg. (EU) 2022/858; MiCA).** Mechanism: a *time-limited exemption regime* — DLT market infrastructures apply to their national competent authority for targeted derogations from named MiFID II/MiFIR and CSDR provisions (notably CSDR's book-entry/CSD requirements), for up to 6 years, subject to compensatory measures and value caps. Most transplantable: statutory power to grant *named exemptions from settlement/depository law* for DLT infrastructures. Indian instrument: a SEBI-administered exemption power under the Depositories Act / SCRA, which SEBI does not currently hold.
- **UK (Digital Securities Sandbox).** Mechanism: under the Financial Services and Markets Act 2023, HM Treasury can *temporarily disapply or modify* named legislation (FSMA 2000 s.20, UK CSDR Art.18 separating CSD and trading-venue functions, the Money Laundering Regs) to let FMIs test DLT; in force 8 Jan 2024 for five years, with the option to exit into a permanently amended framework. Most transplantable — and the cleanest fit for India: a *primary-law power to create financial-market sandboxes that can disapply statute*. Indian instrument: this is the key gap — no Indian statute gives RBI or SEBI a general power to disapply primary legislation for a sandbox; the RBI/SEBI regulatory sandboxes operate only within existing law. Creating one would require an amendment to the RBI Act / SEBI Act (or a dedicated FMI-sandbox Act mirroring FSMA 2023).
- **UAE (ADGM / VARA).** Mechanism: ADGM's Distributed Ledger Technology Foundations Regulations 2023 create a *new legal entity type* (the DLT Foundation) with separate legal personality able to issue tokens and support DAOs under English common law. Most transplantable: a *bespoke legal-entity form* for token-issuing vehicles. Indian instrument: would require a new class under the Companies Act 2013 or an LLP-analogue — low priority for India's institutional case, which is instrument-tokenisation, not DAO governance.
- **US (GENIUS Act, Public Law 119-27, 18 Jul 2025).** Mechanism: a *federal licensing regime for payment stablecoins* — only "permitted payment stablecoin issuers" (bank subsidiaries or OCC-approved nonbanks) may issue, with 1:1 reserves in cash/short T-bills (Treasuries with ≤93-day maturity), no interest to holders; the main provisions take effect the earlier of December 2026 or 120 days after final rules. Most transplantable: a *bank-issued, fully-reserved payment-token licence*. Indian instrument: this maps onto RBI's wholesale-CBDC-settled tokenised deposit model — India is pursuing the CBDC route rather than a private-stablecoin licence, a deliberate divergence.
- **Japan / Hong Kong / Switzerland.** Japan: revised Payment Services Act recognises stablecoins as "electronic payment instruments" issuable by licensed banks/trust companies. Hong Kong: the Stablecoins Ordinance licensing regime plus HKMA's Project Ensemble tokenised-deposit sandbox. Switzerland: the DLT Act (2021) amended the Code of Obligations to create "ledger-based securities" (registered uncertificated securities whose transfer occurs on the ledger). Most transplantable: Switzerland's *ledger-based security* concept — a statutory security type whose legal register *is* the ledger. Indian instrument: an amendment to the SCRA/Depositories Act defining a ledger-based security, which most directly cures India's "ledger-as-register" gap.

## Gap Analysis — could an Indian institution run each Module A "IN PRODUCTION" initiative today?

- **BlackRock BUIDL / Franklin BENJI (tokenised money-market fund on public chain):** No, domestically. Single blocker: VDA tax/classification risk plus no domestic recognition of a public-chain token as a mutual-fund/MMF unit. *In IFSC:* possible under the FME regime + sandbox.
- **JPMorgan Kinexys / deposit tokens:** Partially — RBI's UMI tokenised-CD pilot is the analogue. Single blocker: no settlement-finality statute for on-chain DvP.
- **Citi Token Services / cross-border deposit tokens:** No, domestically (FEMA foreign-currency constraints). *In IFSC:* the intended perimeter.
- **HSBC Orion / sovereign digital bonds:** No. Single blocker: no ledger-as-register recognition; the G-Sec register is RBI's e-Kuber, not a DLT.
- **Broadridge DLR / DTCC-Canton (repo, collateral):** Closest analogue already live is SEBI's DLT covenant system, but repo-on-ledger is not permitted. Single blocker: absence of an EU-DLT-Pilot/UK-DSS-style exemption power.
- **Regulated payment stablecoins (USDC/PYUSD under GENIUS):** No. Single blocker: RBI's policy preference for CBDC over private stablecoins; no licensing framework and active RBI opposition.

## Disconfirming Evidence / What Failed

- **The crypto discussion paper is a five-times-delayed non-event** — an explicit failure of inter-ministerial coordination, with RBI reportedly blocking it (Moneycontrol, Apr 2026). This is the clearest signal that cross-cutting tokenisation policy has *no owner* in India.
- **RBI's own tokenisation officials disclaim legal readiness** — Suvendu Pati's "integrity and enforceability have to be established" and "there needs to be legal changes put in place" is disconfirming of any narrative that India is ready to scale tokenised instruments.
- **The gulf between e-Rupee transaction flow and stock** — ₹1,016 crore in circulation as of March 2025 against cumulative flow claims in the hundreds of millions of transactions — is a candid signal that the CBDC has limited standing usage despite four years of piloting.
- **The 2018 RBI banking ban** is a documented case where the regulator treated the asset as the wrong choice and the courts disagreed — a cautionary precedent that executive prohibition without proportionality fails.
- **India's own Module A graveyard context (IBBIC/IBDIC)** — bank-consortium blockchain trade-finance efforts in India have not reached durable production, mirroring TradeLens/we.trade/Contour globally. (Flagged from Module A; not re-verified here.)
- **No published volumes** for the RBI CD pilot or IFSC tokenised platforms — absence of usage figures is itself disconfirming of "adoption" claims.

## Recommendations

1. **Anchor any institutional pitch on the one production system, not the pilots.** The SEBI/NSDL-CDSL DLT covenant-monitoring platform (4,291 issuers onboarded; ~90% of outstanding secured ISINs mapped, FY25) is the only claim a skeptic cannot dismiss as "announced." Lead with it; treat UMI and the bond pilot as direction-of-travel, labelled as pilots.
2. **For anything requiring on-chain settlement or a ledger-as-register, route through IFSC, not the domestic market.** The TechFin CoR is available now; issuance/platform work goes through the 2026 sandbox. Benchmark that changes this: IFSCA notifying its final RWA tokenisation framework (the Feb 2025 consultation is overdue for follow-through).
3. **Separate the instrument from the asset in every tax conversation.** Seek a CBDT clarification that tokenised securities/deposits are taxed as their underlying instrument. Benchmark: any CBDT circular or Finance Act carve-out narrowing s.2(47A). Until then, assume classificatory risk.
4. **Lobby for the single highest-leverage legal change: a FSMA-2023-style sandbox power.** The UK DSS mechanism (HMT power to disapply/modify named statute for FMIs) is the most transplantable instrument and would cure the settlement-finality and depository-register gaps at once. Benchmark: an amendment to the RBI Act / SEBI Act, or a dedicated FMI-sandbox bill. This is a multi-year ask.
5. **Re-check monthly.** The RBI FY26 report, SEBI's 6-9-month bond-pilot clock (from May 2026, so roughly Nov 2026-Feb 2027), and the IFSCA RWA framework are all live. Anything in this module older than the FY26 RBI report (29 May 2026) should be re-verified before use in a leave-behind.

## Caveats and Staleness

- **Fast-moving:** The RBI CD pilot (Oct 2025), SEBI bond pilot (May 2026), IFSCA sandbox framework (Mar 2026) and the shelved discussion paper (Apr 2026) are all within the six-month window or just outside it; re-check before publication. The SEBI 2022 DLT circulars and the 2020 Supreme Court judgment are settled and stable.
- **Figures I could not source at T1:** No rupee-crore *value* on the DLT covenant platform (only ISIN/issuer counts, and the strongest figures come from vendor Cognizant and NSDL's IPO documents rather than a SEBI statistical release); no participant list or volume for the RBI CD pilot beyond "a few banks"; no tokenised-AUM figure for IFSC. The reported July 2025 "18% GST clarification" rests on advisory blogs and exchange behaviour, not a primary CBIC notification — treated as UNVERIFIED at primary level; what would confirm it is the CBIC circular text.
- **Property ruling reach:** *Rhutikumari* is a single-judge Madras HC decision — persuasive, not binding on other High Courts or nationally; a contrary ruling or Supreme Court view could displace it.
- **CBDC figures:** the transaction counts (120m/175m) and values (₹28,000 crore / ~₹400 billion) come from RBI officials' speeches (T3/T4), not a single audited release, and mix cumulative and point-in-time bases — cite carefully.
- **Conflation warning:** Nearly every popular source conflates the *infrastructure* question (tokenised bonds, deposit tokens, DLT registries) with the *asset* question (private crypto, ETH-as-investment). This module treats them separately, as instructed; where a source blurs them, that is noted.

## Closing Five

**1. The five findings hardest for a skeptic to dismiss.**
- SEBI's DLT covenant-monitoring system is in production, mandatory, and gates ISIN activation (4,291 issuers, ~90% of outstanding secured ISINs mapped, FY25) — hard to dismiss because it rests on a SEBI circular plus the platform vendor's and NSDL's own disclosures, not a promotional claim.
- There is no crypto ban: the Supreme Court set aside the 2018 circular on 4 March 2020 — hard to dismiss because it is a reported judgment.
- Tokenised *securities* are treated as securities, not crypto — hard to dismiss because the Parliamentary Standing Committee said so of the technology-neutral Securities Markets Code 2025.
- The binding constraints are UNADDRESSED gaps (settlement finality, ledger-as-register), not prohibitions — hard to dismiss because RBI's own fintech CGM said the legal changes are not yet in place.
- IFSC already permits the technology layer under a notified 2025 regulation — hard to dismiss because it is a gazetted IFSCA regulation with named sandbox entrants.

**2. Strongest chart.** Adoption of the SEBI DLT Security & Covenant Monitoring System — issuers onboarded and secured/covenant-monitored ISINs as a share of the outstanding universe — pulled from the Cognizant case study (4,291 issuers; 6,368 secured ISINs mapped, ~90% of outstanding; 6,366 trustee-approved, >95%) cross-referenced to NSDL's IPO disclosures. It is the only Indian dataset showing a DLT system in real production use.

**3. Three claims most likely to be challenged, and the best evidence.**
- *"India has a blockchain system live in regulated finance."* Evidence: SEBI Circular /2022/38 of 29 Mar 2022 + Cognizant/NSDL FY25 adoption figures.
- *"Tokenised deposits are being piloted by RBI."* Evidence: RBI Annual Report FY26 (29 May 2026) verbatim on UMI and tokenised CDs; Suvendu Pati interview (7-8 Oct 2025) confirming e₹-W settlement, 1:1 deposit backing, and "a few banks."
- *"Tokenised securities are not taxed as crypto."* Best available (not conclusive): Parliamentary Standing Committee note on the technology-neutral Securities Markets Code 2025; the gap is the absence of an express CBDT carve-out — flagged as classificatory risk.

**4. One-sentence executive summary.** India has quietly put one blockchain registry into mandatory production for corporate bonds and is piloting central-bank-settled tokenised deposits, but the legal plumbing that made tokenisation real in Singapore, the EU, the UK and the US — settlement-finality recognition and a sandbox power to disapply old law — exists in India only inside GIFT City, not in the domestic market.

**5. What this module could not establish, and what would settle it.**
- The *value* (₹ crore) of instruments on the DLT covenant platform — needs an NSDL/CDSL statistical disclosure or an RTI.
- The RBI CD pilot's named participants and volumes — needs an RBI notification or a named-bank disclosure.
- Whether CBDT will treat tokenised securities as non-VDA — needs a CBDT circular or an ITAT/court ruling.
- Whether IFSCA's RWA framework will convert sandbox platforms to full licences — needs the final notified regulation.
- Whether the reported July 2025 18% GST-on-services clarification is a formal CBIC instrument — needs the primary notification.
