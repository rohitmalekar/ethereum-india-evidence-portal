# MODULE D — THE HONEST COMPARISON
### Ethereum versus its realistic alternatives, on the criteria financial institutions actually use

## TL;DR
- On the criteria institutions actually use, Ethereum wins decisively on only three (liveness/uptime, counterparty reachability, and India-available talent) and loses or ties on the criteria that decide most regulated-settlement procurements — confidentiality, cost predictability, permissioning granularity, and legally recognised settlement finality — where permissioned Canton has taken the high-value *multi-party* production book (Broadridge's Distributed Ledger Repo settled ~$7.5tn in June 2026 alone) and permissioned EVM chains took the single-operator cash rails (Kinexys >$3tn cumulative). "Permissioned" is not one stack; the comparison matrix carries all three options.
- Public production data resolves the permissioned-vs-public debate empirically, not rhetorically: funds and stablecoins overwhelmingly chose public Ethereum (~$17.5bn RWA value, ~50% chain share, Aug 2026, RWA.xyz), while every bank-operated core-settlement venue with real volume chose a permissioned ledger, citing sub-transaction privacy as the reason. Ethereum L2s reframe Ethereum's cost problem but do not touch its confidentiality problem.
- For India, Ethereum is the wrong answer wherever confidentiality of positions, a named legal settlement-finality operator, or containment within the RBI/SEBI perimeter is required — which describes nearly every domestic core-market use case today. It is the right answer only for cross-border reach and public-liquidity access, both of which currently sit outside India's permitted perimeter.

## Key Findings

Each finding carries claim, figure with units, "as of" date, source, and source tier (T1 primary / T2 named data provider / T3 tier-1 press / T4 company/promotional / T5 crypto media). Status labels: IN PRODUCTION / LIMITED PILOT / ANNOUNCED ONLY / WOUND DOWN.

### 1. Ethereum's only uncontested institutional advantages are liveness, reach and talent — not the criteria that decide settlement procurement

FINDING: Ethereum mainnet has run since 30 July 2015 with no recorded chain halt through 15+ upgrades and a live proof-of-work-to-proof-of-stake switch (as of Aug 2026; ethereumuptime.com incident log, 11 entries / 0 minutes downtime — T5, corroborated by ethereum.org — T4). Solana, by contrast, had at least 11 partial or full network outages between 2020 and early 2024 (Helius, "A Complete History of Solana Outages"), the last major halt being ~5 hours on 6 Feb 2024, followed by 16+ consecutive months without a major outage as of mid-2025 (MEXC/analyst commentary — T5). Ethereum's uptime is a genuine, checkable advantage and the reason "liveness" appears on institutional shortlists.
INDIAN ANALOGUE: NSDL/CDSL depository uptime and RBI RTGS/NEFT availability — both already very high, so the marginal liveness gain for a domestic issuer is small.
WHO WOULD OWN IT: A depository (NSDL/CDSL) CTO function, or a clearing corporation (NSE Clearing / Indian Clearing Corporation) operations head.
WHAT BLOCKS IT TODAY: No statute recognises a DLT ledger as the legal securities register (Depositories Act 1996 contemplates a depository); liveness is not the binding constraint.
WHAT WOULD HAVE TO CHANGE: Amendment to the Depositories Act or an enabling SEBI regulation recognising a distributed register — a legislative/SEBI act, not a technical one.
CONFIDENCE: High that uptime is real; high that it is not the deciding criterion in India.

### 2. Confidentiality is the criterion Ethereum loses on, and it is the reason banks chose Canton

FINDING: On Ethereum mainnet and its L2s today, institutional tokenised assets settle on fully public ledgers. The dominant compliance pattern — BlackRock's BUIDL — is a permissioned/whitelisted ERC-20: it gates who may hold or transfer, but balances, transfers, mints, redemptions and holder addresses are all publicly visible on Etherscan (BUIDL contract 0x7712c34205737192402172409a8f7ccef8aa2aec; ~60 holders visible, 22 Jul 2026 — T1 on-chain). Whitelisting is access control, not confidentiality. The purpose-built Ethereum privacy layer that came closest, Aztec Connect, was WOUND DOWN (deposits disabled 31 Mar 2023, sequencer retired 31 Mar 2024; Aztec Labs blog — T4). Its successor, Aztec "Ignition," reached mainnet only in Nov 2025 (The Block — T3), produced empty blocks at launch with user transactions enabled in early 2026, and has no documented regulated-FI production deployment. EY Nightfall_4 (ZK rollup, public-domain code, Apr 2025 — T1/T4 EY) exists but has no confirmed named regulated-FI Ethereum-mainnet securities production use.
By contrast, Canton Network provides protocol-level sub-transaction privacy (each party sees only its slice of a transaction), which DTCC, Goldman's GS DAP and HSBC Orion cited as the explicit reason for choosing it over public Ethereum. Digital Asset CEO Yuval Rooz (Dec 2025, The Defiant — T3): institutions "can't have their positions visible to the entire world in real time." HSBC's Tokenized Deposit Service launched May 2025 on Orion using Canton, chosen for "configurable privacy" (Ledger Insights — T3).
INDIAN ANALOGUE: Corporate-bond OTC block trades and repo positions, where a dealer's inventory being publicly visible in real time would move the market against them; SEBI's DLT covenant-monitoring system already keeps issuer data private.
WHO WOULD OWN IT: A bank treasury/markets desk (e.g., SBI, HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank — supervised by RBI) or a primary-dealer fixed-income desk.
WHAT BLOCKS IT TODAY: Public-chain transparency is commercially unacceptable for position-sensitive trading; no Indian regulation forces it — the market structure does.
WHAT WOULD HAVE TO CHANGE: Either production-grade privacy shipping on an Ethereum L2 and being sanctioned by SEBI/RBI, or the domestic pilots choosing a permissioned ledger (as RBI's UMI and the SEBI bond pilot effectively do).
CONFIDENCE: High. This is the single most decisive and best-evidenced finding in the module.

### 3. Cost is low on average but volatile — and volatility, not average, is what a treasurer prices

FINDING: Ethereum mainnet base fees fell to roughly 0.05–0.7 gwei through 2026 (avg ~$0.16–$0.22/tx, Mar 2026; CoinLaw — T5, corroborated by Etherscan-derived figures), a ~95% drop since the Dencun/EIP-4844 upgrade (Mar 2024). But fees remain spiky: during the Oct 2025 flash drop, fees briefly hit ~15.9 gwei before falling below 1 gwei (T5). Historically, gas hit 480 gwei (2020) and $53/tx average (May 2021). L2s (Arbitrum ~$0.004–0.09, Base ~$0.02) are cheaper but add sequencer and bridge dependency. Permissioned ledgers (Canton, Corda, Fabric) have deterministic, contractually fixed operating costs — no public fee auction.
INDIAN ANALOGUE: UPI runs at effectively zero marginal cost to users at 24.51bn transactions/month (Aug 2026, ₹29.82 lakh crore ≈ US$339bn at ₹88/US$; NPCI via Business Standard — T3); any per-transaction fee auction is a non-starter for high-volume domestic retail rails.
WHO WOULD OWN IT: A payments/operations CFO function at a bank or at NPCI.
WHAT BLOCKS IT TODAY: Fee unpredictability is a budgeting and worst-case-provisioning problem, not a legal one; for securities settlement, volumes are low enough that cost is rarely binding.
WHAT WOULD HAVE TO CHANGE: Stable, capped fee arrangements (as BlackRock negotiated subsidised 20bps management fees on Aptos/Avalanche/Polygon) or use of a permissioned chain with fixed costs.
CONFIDENCE: High that average cost is low; high that volatility is the more relevant metric for treasurers.

### 4. The permissioned book is real, large, and concentrated — but concentrated in one application

FINDING: Broadridge's Distributed Ledger Repo (DLR), a Canton-lineage permissioned platform, reported ~$7.3tn settled in January 2026 (508% YoY, ~$365bn average daily volume), ~$8tn in March 2026 (~$354bn ADV), and ~$7.5tn in June 2026 (~$357bn ADV), per Broadridge's own press releases (NYSE:BR — T4/company; DLR data now published on Bloomberg Terminal, ticker BDLRTRDC, via Kaiko — T2); Module A cited ~$8.0tn for July 2026. This is up from ~$2tn/month in 2024. RWA.xyz attributes ~$344.8bn represented asset value on Canton (12 May 2026 — T2), but DefiLlama placed Canton public TVL at ~$961,000 (Jun 2026 — T2): the value is institutionally controlled, not publicly liquid. Critically, most Canton throughput is a single application (DLR repo), a market-structure artefact — repeated overnight collateral cycles inflate gross volume — rather than diversified on-chain activity (Rosa & Roubini, May 2026 — T3).
INDIAN ANALOGUE: The tri-party repo (TREPS) and CROMS/CCIL-settled G-sec repo markets — high-volume, collateral-driven, already centrally settled by CCIL.
WHO WOULD OWN IT: Clearing Corporation of India Ltd (CCIL), supervised by RBI.
WHAT BLOCKS IT TODAY: CCIL already provides guaranteed central settlement; a DLT repo layer adds little unless it enables intraday collateral mobility CCIL cannot.
WHAT WOULD HAVE TO CHANGE: RBI would have to identify a specific collateral-velocity gain and sanction a pilot; the Payment and Settlement Systems Act 2007 covers only RBI-designated systems, so on-chain DvP finality has no domestic statutory basis.
CONFIDENCE: High on the volume figures; medium on interpreting how transferable the model is to India.

### 5. The consortium graveyard implies permissioned networks fail on governance and network effect, not technology

FINDING: Module A's graveyard — TradeLens (WOUND DOWN Q1 2023, failed on neutral-governance grounds), we.trade (2022, insufficient network effect), Marco Polo (2023, no product-market fit), Contour (late 2023), ASX CHESS/Digital Asset Daml (scrapped Nov 2022, ~A$250m write-off, "significant technology, governance and delivery challenges") — is overwhelmingly a graveyard of permissioned/consortium DLT, not public-chain projects. R3 Corda's "growth has plateaued and its open-source community has contracted" (Cypherock — T5); R3 announced a Solana partnership in 2025 to access public liquidity (Coinpaprika — T5), a tacit admission that closed networks struggle for reach. This is the disconfirming evidence the permissioned camp must answer: the technology worked; the governance and liquidity did not.
INDIAN ANALOGUE: IBBIC/IBDIC (15–18 bank consortium on Infosys Finacle Connect, LIMITED PILOT, passed RBI Sandbox 2025) is structurally identical to the failed consortia — a bank-owned network needing every rival to route trade-finance data through shared infrastructure.
WHO WOULD OWN IT: The IBDIC consortium banks jointly; no single budget-holder, which is precisely the governance weakness.
WHAT BLOCKS IT TODAY: The same neutral-governance and network-effect problem that killed TradeLens and we.trade.
WHAT WOULD HAVE TO CHANGE: A genuinely neutral operator or regulator-run utility model (the UPI/NPCI template), not a bank-owned consortium.
CONFIDENCE: High. The failure pattern is consistent and well-documented.

### 6. Regulatory recognition favours the asset/issuer layer, and is chain-agnostic where it exists

FINDING: Where regulators have sanctioned tokenisation, they have regulated the issuer and the instrument, not blessed a chain. The US GENIUS Act (signed 18 Jul 2025, Public Law 119-27 — T1) regulates payment-stablecoin issuers; EU MiCA (full regime from 30 Dec 2024, transition ended 1 Jul 2026 — T1) regulates EMTs/ARTs. Neither privileges Ethereum. DTCC received an SEC No-Action Letter to tokenise DTC-custodied assets (Dec 2025 — T1) and chose Canton. In India, SEBI's DLT covenant-monitoring system (NSDL/CDSL, mandated 29 Mar 2022) is IN PRODUCTION (4,291 issuers, ~90% secured ISINs mapped, FY25); RBI's tokenised-CD pilot on UMI settled via wholesale CBDC (e₹-W) is LIMITED PILOT (8 Oct 2025); SEBI's corporate-bond tokenisation pilot is ANNOUNCED ONLY (26 May 2026, first REC issuance floated for Sep 2026 at sub-₹500 crore ≈ sub-US$57m; 6–9 month window per SEBI Chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey, Business Standard — T3). All three are chain-agnostic or lean permissioned/CBDC-integrated — none is on public Ethereum.
INDIAN ANALOGUE: The SEBI bond pilot and RBI UMI pilot are the direct analogues; both integrate with CBDC settlement rather than a public chain.
WHO WOULD OWN IT: SEBI (market instruments) and RBI (settlement/CBDC), the two named regulators.
WHAT BLOCKS IT TODAY: No settlement-finality statute for on-chain DvP; VDA tax (30% flat, s.115BBH, no loss set-off) creates classificatory risk that a tokenised security could be treated as a VDA.
WHAT WOULD HAVE TO CHANGE: A carve-out clarifying that tokenised regulated securities are not VDAs (CBDT/Finance Ministry), and a settlement-finality provision (RBI/Parliament).
CONFIDENCE: High.

### 7. Talent in India favours EVM/Solidity — an Ethereum advantage — but not for permissioned stacks

FINDING: India onboarded 17% of the 39,148 new crypto developers globally in 2024 — the most of any country — rising from 10th to 2nd overall (Electric Capital 2024 Developer Report, based on 902 million code commits across 1.7 million repos — T2). India's share of global Web3 developers rose to 15.2%, "the fastest-growing developer ecosystem globally and the only major market trending upward as US activity declines," per Hashed Emergent's 4th-edition India Web3 Landscape Report (data current as of March 2026 — T3/T4). The skills are concentrated in Solidity/EVM and Rust/Solana — a genuine Ethereum-family advantage, since EVM talent is abundant. But Canton/Daml and Corda skills are scarce everywhere, and a 2023 NASSCOM projection of needing 800,000+ blockchain experts by 2026 (Outlook — T4) points to a shortage at the enterprise/permissioned end.
INDIAN ANALOGUE: The IT-services majors (TCS, Infosys — Finacle already underpins IBDIC) hold enterprise DLT talent; the startup/DeFi pool holds the EVM talent.
WHO WOULD OWN IT: Bank technology procurement and the IT-services vendors.
WHAT BLOCKS IT TODAY: For a permissioned deployment, Daml/Corda talent must be trained or bought; for a public-Ethereum deployment, compliance/legal capacity is the bottleneck, not developers.
WHAT WOULD HAVE TO CHANGE: Vendor training programmes (Infosys/TCS) for permissioned stacks; regulatory clarity to let the abundant EVM pool work on sanctioned domestic projects.
CONFIDENCE: Medium-high; developer-count figures vary by methodology and source tier.

### 8. Security: Ethereum's base layer is durable; the bridges connecting chains are the systemic weak point

FINDING: Ethereum's base layer has never been halted, but the multi-chain architecture institutions adopt to reach it is the single largest theft category. Chainalysis (2 Aug 2022) documented "$2 billion in cryptocurrency … stolen across 13 separate cross-chain bridge hacks," with bridges accounting for 69% of total funds stolen in 2022 to that point (Ronin ~$624m, Wormhole ~$320m, Nomad ~$190m — T2). BUIDL's own multi-chain design relies on Wormhole for cross-chain messaging (T4/T5) — the same Wormhole that lost $326m in Feb 2022. Chainalysis's 2026 Crypto Crime Report (18 Dec 2025) put full-year 2025 theft at "more than $3.4 billion … stolen from January through early December 2025," with the top three hacks representing 69% of losses and North Korea-linked actors taking $2.02bn (T2). Permissioned ledgers with no public bridge and known participants have a materially smaller external attack surface.
INDIAN ANALOGUE: Any Indian multi-chain tokenisation design (e.g., issuing on both a domestic chain and a GIFT City chain) would inherit bridge risk.
WHO WOULD OWN IT: Bank/depository CISO and operational-risk functions.
WHAT BLOCKS IT TODAY: Bridge risk is an operational-risk and capital-charge concern; Basel's punitive treatment of permissionless-chain exposures already discourages it.
WHAT WOULD HAVE TO CHANGE: Single-chain issuance (no bridge) or ZK/optimistic bridge designs proven at institutional scale, which are not yet deployed there.
CONFIDENCE: High on the bridge-loss figures; the base-layer/bridge distinction is important and often elided.

**Why the permissioned-EVM column exists, and how to read it.** The public-vs-Canton framing that dominates this debate omits the option a large part of the production book actually runs on: a permissioned Ethereum-lineage chain. JPMorgan's Kinexys — >$3tn cumulative, ~$5–7bn/day (Module A) — is Quorum-derived, and Citi CTS is a bank-built private chain, so the "banks chose Canton" shorthand is true of the *securities and collateral* layer and false of the *cash* layer. Two caveats, stated plainly. First, the architectural cells in this column (consensus, privacy groups, allowlisting) are drawn from Besu/Quorum client documentation at T4 and should be cited to those docs, not to this table; **no named regulated-FI deployment of multi-party confidential securities settlement on a permissioned EVM was found**, which is the whole reason Canton holds that layer. Second, **Kinexys' current stack is UNVERIFIED**: the Quorum lineage is documented for Onyx, but the Nov 2024 rebrand may have brought undisclosed changes, and Citi has never disclosed CTS's technology. Confirm with a JPMorgan or Citi disclosure before citing the lineage externally.

**The mechanism, since it is the part usually asserted rather than explained.** Canton keeps no global state: each participant node stores only its own projection of a contract, and a global synchroniser orders and proves atomicity across those projections without any node seeing the whole. Privacy is the data model. A permissioned EVM replicates global state across the permissioned set and then bolts privacy on as private transaction groups — side databases shared pairwise or among a small set. The consequence decides procurements: private state does not compose across groups, and atomic DvP breaks the moment one trade spans more than one privacy group. That is exactly the shape of dealer-to-dealer repo and depository settlement — many counterparties, one atomic transaction, each seeing only its own leg — so Canton's win there is an engineering result, not vendor spin. It is also why a permissioned EVM is sufficient where Kinexys sits: one bank, one balance sheet, participants who are clients rather than each other's counterparties, and a privacy boundary that is simply the institution's perimeter. *(Architecture as described in Digital Asset's Canton documentation and Besu/Quorum client documentation — T4; needs direct citation to those primary docs before external use.)*

### Which L2s have real institutional usage (with evidence)

- **Arbitrum** — IN PRODUCTION as an RWA venue: ~$0.85bn distributed RWA value, 10,713 RWA holders (RWA.xyz, Aug 2026, T2); BUIDL share class live (50bps, same as mainnet). Real but small relative to mainnet.
- **Base** (Coinbase, OP Stack) — largest L2 by activity (~12.89m tx/day, ~$12bn TVL — T5); JPMorgan chose Base to host its JPM Coin/Kinexys deposit token natively (T5), a notable institutional signal, but Base's RWA book is modest.
- **Optimism (OP Mainnet)** — BUIDL share class live (T4); low RWA value.
- **ZKsync Era** — ~$0.96bn distributed RWA (RWA.xyz, T2), described as institutionally focused, but holder count is tiny (266) — this is issuance parked, not liquidity.
- **Polygon** — BUIDL/BENJI share classes live at subsidised 20bps (BlackRock PR, T4); meaningful issuance, thin secondary activity.

The honest read: L2 institutional "usage" is overwhelmingly issuance of share classes that mirror mainnet, driven by foundation fee subsidies (Aptos Foundation, Avalanche (BVI) Inc. and Polygon Labs BD Investments each pay BlackRock a quarterly fee — BlackRock PR, T4), not by independent institutional liquidity. Only Base has a distinct institutional anchor (JPM Coin/Kinexys), and that is a permissioned deposit token, not open DeFi.

### Privacy: shipped cryptography versus papers (the decisive distinction)

In production on Ethereum for regulated institutional transactions:
- **CAN** (shipped): access control / whitelisting at the ERC-20 level (BUIDL registry contract, Securitize-maintained); freeze/allow-list compliance.
- **CANNOT** (in production): hide balances, amounts, counterparties or holder identities on the base chain (all public on Etherscan, T1); offer protocol-native selective-disclosure privacy comparable to Canton. Aztec Connect was WOUND DOWN (2023–24); Aztec Ignition reached mainnet Nov 2025 (The Block, T3) but enabled user transactions only in early 2026 and has no documented regulated-FI production use; EY Nightfall_4 is real open-source ZK tooling (EY, Apr 2025, T1) but has no confirmed named regulated-FI Ethereum-mainnet securities production deployment.

Canton's sub-transaction privacy is IN PRODUCTION and is the stated reason DTCC (SEC No-Action Letter, Dec 2025, T1 — MVP targeted H1 2026, so the DTCC deployment itself is LIMITED PILOT / ANNOUNCED), Goldman GS DAP (IN PRODUCTION since late 2022; spin-out ANNOUNCED Nov 2024), and HSBC Orion (Tokenized Deposit Service IN PRODUCTION May 2025) chose it. A skeptical counterpoint for balance: critics argue Canton is effectively a permissioned DAG "with no globally verifiable state," trading public auditability for privacy (Status Network commentary, Dec 2025, T5).

## Where Ethereum is the wrong answer — six concrete institutional scenarios

1. **Dealer-to-dealer corporate-bond and repo settlement where positions are market-sensitive.** A public ledger exposes inventory. This is precisely why Broadridge DLR (~$7.5tn, June 2026) runs on permissioned Canton, not Ethereum. For India's OTC corporate-bond market (₹53.6 lakh crore outstanding, FY25), a public chain is the wrong choice; a permissioned ledger or CCIL-operated system is correct. WHY: confidentiality is a hard requirement, and Ethereum cannot provide it in production.

2. **Any core settlement that must have a named, accountable legal operator with statutory settlement finality.** India's PSS Act 2007 designates specific RBI-run systems; a leaderless public chain has no operator to designate and no statutory finality. A conventional RBI/CCIL system, or at most a permissioned ledger with a named operator, is right here. WHY: legal finality attaches to an operator, not to probabilistic consensus.

3. **High-volume domestic retail payments.** UPI already clears 24.51bn tx/month at ~zero marginal cost with legally recognised settlement (NPCI, Aug 2026). No public chain offers cheaper, faster, or more legally certain domestic retail settlement; putting UPI-scale flow on Ethereum would be strictly worse on cost, finality and governance. WHY: a well-run centralised utility dominates on every criterion that matters here.

4. **Consent-based data sharing and identity for lending.** India's Account Aggregator framework already moves financial data via consent (284.6m linked accounts against 2.88bn enabled, Mar 2026 — IMPRI/RBI, T3/T1). A blockchain adds no benefit and removes the confidentiality and regulatory containment the AA design provides. WHY: the problem is consent and data-minimisation, not shared-ledger consensus.

5. **A bank-owned consortium trying to onboard competitors onto shared trade-finance infrastructure.** The graveyard (TradeLens, we.trade, Marco Polo, Contour, ASX CHESS) shows this fails on governance and network effect regardless of chain, and IBBIC/IBDIC faces the same risk. Neither public Ethereum nor a permissioned consortium solves the neutral-governance problem; a regulator-run or genuinely neutral utility is needed. WHY: the binding constraint is trust in the operator, not the ledger technology.

6. **When a conventional shared database with strong controls suffices.** For a single depository maintaining a securities register (NSDL/CDSL), a well-controlled database with audit logging already delivers the outcome. SEBI's DLT covenant-monitoring system is the narrow case where a shared ledger across NSDL/CDSL and issuers adds value — and even that is a permissioned, regulator-mandated design, not public Ethereum.

## The permissioned-vs-public question, resolved by the production record

The evidence, not argument, resolves it: the market has already bifurcated by use case.
- **Public Ethereum won the funds-and-stablecoins layer** — assets whose holders benefit from public composability, 24/7 transfer, and broad reachability (BUIDL, BENJI, USDC). Ethereum holds ~50% of the ~$33.5–38bn tracked RWA value (RWA.xyz, Jul–Aug 2026).
- **Permissioned Canton won the bank-and-market-infrastructure layer** — repo, collateral, tokenised deposits, and depository settlement, where confidentiality and a named operator are non-negotiable (Broadridge DLR ~$7.5tn/June 2026; DTCC, GS DAP, HSBC Orion).

**Do L2s change the trade-off or merely reframe it? They reframe the cost problem without touching the confidentiality problem.** L2s cut fees by 90%+ and add throughput, addressing Ethereum's weakest *cost* criterion. But they inherit the base layer's public transparency: an L2 transaction is still visible, and the privacy L2s (Aztec) are not in regulated production. So for the banks that chose Canton for privacy, L2s do not change the decision. For the funds that chose public Ethereum for reach, L2s make the choice cheaper. The bifurcation persists.

**What the consortium graveyard implies for permissioned durability:** permissioned ledgers are not technically fragile — they are *governance*-fragile. The survivors (Canton, Broadridge DLR) had either a neutral foundation (Canton Foundation, with DTCC/Euroclear co-chairs) or a single credible operator (Broadridge). The ones that died (TradeLens, we.trade, Marco Polo) had bank-consortium governance with no neutral party. The lesson for India's IBDIC is direct: the technology is not the risk; the ownership structure is.

## Falsifiable conditions under which Ethereum would be the wrong long-term bet for institutional settlement

Stated as checkable conditions, not vague risks:
1. **If a permissioned ledger captures >70% of tokenised-RWA *value* (not just repo throughput) by 2028** — i.e. Canton or a successor overtakes Ethereum in the funds/credit layer, not just collateral — then public Ethereum's reachability advantage will have been beaten on its home turf. Check: RWA.xyz chain-share series.
2. **If production-grade confidential transactions do NOT ship and get regulator-sanctioned on any Ethereum L2 by end-2027**, the confidentiality gap becomes structural, not temporary, and the banks' Canton choice becomes permanent. Check: whether Aztec (or equivalent) has a named regulated-FI deployment.
3. **If single-slot finality is not delivered and a competing chain offers both sub-second deterministic finality and confidentiality in production**, Ethereum loses on two criteria simultaneously. Check: Ethereum roadmap delivery vs. Solana/Canton finality claims.
4. **If a major bridge failure causes a systemic loss in tokenised institutional assets**, the multi-chain model that lets institutions reach Ethereum becomes uninsurable, pushing issuance to single permissioned ledgers. Check: bridge-loss series and any tokenised-RWA-specific incident.
5. **If regulators (SEBI/RBI, or globally) mandate that regulated securities settle only on operator-accountable systems with statutory finality**, public Ethereum is excluded by rule regardless of merit. Check: settlement-finality legislation.

A sixth condition runs the other way — it falsifies the *Canton* case rather than the Ethereum one, and it belongs here because the module's central claim is that confidentiality is an architectural loss, not a temporary one:

6. **If a named regulated FI puts multi-party confidential securities settlement into production on a permissioned EVM by end-2027**, the privacy argument for Canton collapses from an architectural argument to a convenience one — because the thing Canton is said to do that an EVM cannot (many counterparties, one atomic transaction, each seeing only its own leg) would have been done on an EVM. That would also make the permissioned-EVM option strictly dominant for India, since it already wins on talent and migration optionality and would no longer lose on privacy. Check: any production (not pilot) deployment on Besu/Quorum-lineage infrastructure settling a multi-party trade with per-party confidentiality, named FI and named instrument; today no such deployment was found, and private transaction groups do not compose across groups, which is the technical reason to expect this condition to stay unmet.

If none become true by ~2028, Ethereum's institutional position at the asset layer is durable; if two or more become true, the long-term bet weakens materially.

## Recommendations

Staged, with thresholds that would change them.

**Stage 1 (now – 6 months): Separate the two questions in every internal paper.** Treat "public chain for reach" and "permissioned ledger for confidential settlement" as two different procurements with different owners. Do not let a single vendor pitch conflate them. Threshold to revisit: an Ethereum L2 ships regulator-sanctioned confidential settlement.

**Stage 2 (6–18 months): Match each domestic use case to the correct rail.**
- Corporate-bond OTC/repo settlement → permissioned ledger with a named operator (align with the SEBI bond pilot and RBI UMI/CBDC integration), NOT public Ethereum. Threshold: reconsider only if privacy ships and is sanctioned on Ethereum.
- Fund tokenisation for GIFT City / cross-border distribution → public Ethereum is defensible, because reach and 24/7 transfer are the point and confidentiality matters less for a fund share. Threshold: proceed only within the IFSCA sandbox until a VDA-classification carve-out exists.
- Retail payments, data-sharing, identity → keep on UPI / AA / DigiLocker; do not blockchain-ify. Threshold: none foreseeable.

**Stage 3 (ongoing): Run the five falsifiable conditions above as a monitoring dashboard.** If confidentiality ships on an L2 and gets sanctioned, escalate public-chain settlement from "wrong answer" to "candidate." If a bridge failure hits tokenised assets, freeze multi-chain designs.

**Governance rule for any Indian consortium (IBDIC and successors):** do not launch bank-owned-consortium governance. Insist on a neutral operator or regulator-utility model (the UPI/NPCI template), or expect the TradeLens/we.trade outcome.

## The five standard closing items

**1. The five findings hardest to dismiss, and why.**
- (a) BUIDL's holder list and transfers are public on Etherscan (contract 0x7712…2aec) — checkable in one click; it proves whitelisting ≠ confidentiality. Hard to dismiss because it is primary on-chain data.
- (b) Broadridge DLR settled ~$7.5tn in June 2026 on permissioned Canton, not Ethereum — the largest tokenised-settlement book chose privacy over public chain. Hard to dismiss because the number is large and the venue choice is explicit.
- (c) The consortium graveyard is overwhelmingly permissioned DLT (TradeLens, we.trade, Marco Polo, Contour, ASX CHESS) — disconfirms the "permissioned is safer" reflex on governance grounds. Hard to dismiss because it is a pattern, not one case.
- (d) Ethereum has never halted since 2015 while Solana had ≥11 outages through early 2024 — a genuine, checkable Ethereum advantage the skeptic will respect precisely because it is conceded elsewhere.
- (e) Cross-chain bridges leaked $2bn across 13 hacks in 2022 alone (69% of that year's stolen funds) — the multi-chain reach that makes Ethereum attractive is also its largest attack surface. Hard to dismiss because the losses are documented and recurring.
